# Closing Rings

An algorithmic philosophy for the labs' orrery.

A world is an ink ring suspended in fog, and the ring is only as closed as the work is finished. The arc a world
draws equals the share of its labs that have reached their fiction made real; the rest of the circle stays open, and
the opening is the honest picture of what is missing. Nothing on the figure is decoration: every arc length, every
filament and every fleck of drifting ink is computed from the labs' measured levels, so the image cannot flatter.

Each lab leaves its ring as a single brushed filament whose reach is its graded level on a scale of ten. A dotted
threshold circles every world at level five, the line that one finished product has already crossed. Filaments that
are live on the site burn gold, the only warm colour in the system and reserved for what has actually fired; the
rest are drawn in cold ink. A filament at zero is a bare notch in the ring: a lab that is named and not yet begun.

Ink is shed continuously from the rings and filaments and carried by a divergence-free curl field, so it swirls without
ever pooling or vanishing into sinks, the way dye moves in still water. Each frame paints a faint veil of the ground
over the last instead of clearing it, so every particle leaves a smoke trail that thins and dies over a second or two.
The field drifts slowly in time; the composition is never the same twice, yet a given seed always yields the same
world.

The figure ignites rather than appears: the rings draw themselves to their measured closure, the filaments extend to
their levels, and only then does the ink begin to move. Under reduced motion the ignition is skipped and the finished
frame is drawn once, still and complete. The algorithm should read as the product of patient, master-level tuning:
the veil's opacity, the field's scale, the brush's taper and the particle budget balanced so that the motion feels
like breath and never like a screensaver.

Seven worlds orbit the hub with sevenfold symmetry. A closed ring has no beginning and no end.
