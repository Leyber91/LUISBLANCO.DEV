Which rocky worlds keep air

The survey draws every rocky planet in one of three ways: as bare rock, with thin air, or with thick air. This page says how it chooses, from the code that does it (labs/assets/js/exoplanet/air.js and data.js). Every paper below was opened and read on 16 November 2026. real

Observed first, then extrapolated

real Each rocky world carries three scenarios. Each scenario has a status: observed; excluded, when a paper rules it out for every composition it considers, or by energy balance (the day side is as hot as bare rock, and air that thick would carry heat to the night side whatever its gases; Koll 2022); disfavoured, when a paper rules out part of it, some pressures or some gases, so that it stays possible; or allowed. The statuses are worked out by the code from each paper's bounds, written down as data: a range of surface pressure, the gases it covers, the paper's words and where they are.

Bare rock
No atmosphere.
Thin air
An atmosphere too thin to carry the day side's heat to the night side. From outside it looks like bare rock, unless it holds a gas with a strong band in the filter, such as CO2 at 15 µm.
Thick air
An atmosphere that carries heat to the night side, or a deck of cloud. Koll (2022) finds that this starts once the surface pressure passes about 1 bar.
  1. Observed first. A rocky planet with its own published observation has each scenario set by its paper's bounds. The picture draws the observed scenario if there is one; else the one the paper concludes for, unless the paper excludes it; else, by the fit, the most probable among those the paper leaves open. An allowed scenario comes before a disfavoured one.
  2. Extrapolated for the rest. Every other rocky planet is drawn by the fit of Berta-Thompson, Wachiraphan & Murray (2026), which gives the probability P that a planet kept an atmosphere. Its labels are mixed: a Solar System body counts as having one with any air above 10−6 bar, so Mars counts, and an exoplanet when its eclipse shows one, which for these planets means air thicker than about 1 to 10 bar ("probably ≲ 10 bar CO2" is how the paper reads its "no atmosphere"). So the fit learns from Solar System bodies with any air and from exoplanets whose eclipses show a thick one, and it does not say how thick. The picture draws bare rock when P is under one half, and air otherwise. illustrative Thin or thick is the survey's own rule: the amount of air it drew before this rule, escape speed against sunlight, anchored at 7 km/s, which gives Mars 0.47 of Earth's air and Venus 0.82. Thick air for every P over one half would contradict Mars and give every hot rock of a Sun-like star thick air, which nothing measured supports.
  3. Every scenario that is not excluded can be drawn. Under the planet's name, a short line says what is drawn and the chance of air, and its scenarios and sources list the three with their status; each one that can be drawn instead is a link, where the browser can build its look away from the page's thread or has built it already. An excluded scenario is listed as excluded, with its reason here, and is never drawn.

A world drawn with a surface is one whose radius class is rocky or super-Earth in data.js; a sub-Neptune or anything larger is drawn as cloud tops and has no scenarios.

What an eclipse can see

real Almost every observation here is an eclipse: the planet passes behind its star and the drop in light is the planet's own glow. That gives the temperature of the day side. A rock with no atmosphere re-emits sunlight where it lands, so its day side is as hot as it can be: Tday = Tss (2/3)1/4, where Tss is the temperature under the star (Koll 2022, eq. 1). A thick atmosphere moves heat to the night side and the day side comes out cooler. 55 Cnc e is the clearest case: its day side is 1,796 ± 88 K against 2,511 ± 26 K for a black rock with no redistribution (Hu et al. 2024).

real A thin atmosphere moves little heat, so its day side is about as hot as bare rock and the eclipse cannot tell them apart. The papers can rule one out only when it would hold a gas that absorbs inside the filter. So they rule out thin atmospheres of CO2, down to 10 mbar of pure CO2 on LHS 1140 c (Fortune et al. 2025) and 0.01 bar on LTT 3780 b (Allen et al. 2025), and leave thin atmospheres without it open: oxygen on LTT 3780 b, and about 0.1 bar on GJ 367 b (Zhang et al. 2024). The transits of TRAPPIST-1 d and e are limited by light from the star's own surface that changes from one visit to the next (Piaulet-Ghorayeb et al. 2025; Glidden et al. 2025).

That is why thin air is excluded below only on TOI-561 b, whose day side is too cool for any thin atmosphere. Where a paper rules out thin CO2, thin air is disfavoured and stays drawable. Most bounds on thick air are of the same kind: they cover the gases a paper modelled, or pressures from 10 bar up, and thick air is excluded only where a paper rules it out for every composition or by energy balance: on GJ 367 b and GJ 486 b, whose day sides are as hot as bare rock.

Seventeen rocky planets with their own observation

real How the list was made. Section II.2 of the 2026 fit paper names the rocky exoplanets with an atmospheric constraint: 13 it labels as having none (LHS 3844 b, GJ 367 b, TOI-1685 b, GJ 1252 b, TOI-1468 b, LHS 1140 c, LTT 3780 b, GJ 3929 b, GJ 486 b, GJ 1132 b, LTT 1445 A b, TRAPPIST-1 b and c) and 2 as having one (55 Cnc e, TOI-561 b). The survey adds TRAPPIST-1 d and e, whose JWST transmission spectra were published in 2025. Each planet's own paper was then opened and read: its abstract for every entry, and its text for the numbers that are not in the abstract (55 Cnc e, GJ 367 b, TOI-561 b and the 2026 paper on LHS 3844 b). The paper lists six more with an observation and no verdict (Kepler-10 b, Kepler-78 b, K2-141 b, LHS 1478 b, TOI-431 b, L 98-59 b); their papers were not read for this table, so the fit decides them.

TRAPPIST-1 b

Drawn as bare rock, by the paper's conclusion. Look: airless rock.

The day side re-emits the starlight it takes in, with no CO2 band seen; the simplest reading is little or no atmosphere (Greene 2023). With a second filter, an airless planet with a fresh dark surface fits, and so does a thick pure-CO2 atmosphere whose haze inverts its temperature (Ducrot 2025). The phase curve gives a day side of 490 ± 17 K, no night side detected, and disfavours atmospheres of 1 bar or more with an efficient greenhouse effect (Gillon 2025, a preprint).

  • Bare rock: allowed, drawn. No bound of Greene 2023 and Ducrot 2025 and Gillon 2025 reaches it. The paper concludes: “The most straightforward interpretation is that there is little or no planetary atmosphere redistributing radiation from the host star” (Greene 2023).
  • Thin air: allowed. No bound of Greene 2023 and Ducrot 2025 and Gillon 2025 reaches it.
  • Thick air: disfavoured. Atmospheres of 1 bar or more with an efficient greenhouse effect are strongly disfavoured (Gillon 2025, a preprint).
  • “The most straightforward interpretation is that there is little or no planetary atmosphere redistributing radiation from the host star” (Greene et al. 2023, abstract)
  • “Atmosphere models with surface pressures ≥1 bar and efficient greenhouse effects are strongly disfavoured for both planets.” (Gillon et al. 2025, abstract)

Day side measured 490 K (−17, +17; Gillon et al. 2025); drawn at 490 K.

TRAPPIST-1 e

Drawn as bare rock, by the fit: a 1% chance of air. Look: airless rock.

Four transits with strong contamination from the star itself, which varied between visits. Cloudy hydrogen atmospheres (80% or more by volume) are excluded at more than 3 σ (Espinoza 2025). There is no strong evidence for or against an atmosphere: CO2-rich ones at the pressures of Venus and Mars are weakly disfavoured at 2 σ, and N2-rich ones with traces of CO2 and CH4 are permitted; a bare rock and an N2 atmosphere both fit (Glidden 2025).

  • Bare rock: allowed, drawn. No bound of Espinoza 2025 and Glidden 2025 reaches it.
  • Thin air: disfavoured. CO2-rich atmospheres at the surface pressures of Mars and Venus (about 0.006 and 92 bar) are weakly disfavoured at 2 σ (Glidden 2025).
  • Thick air: disfavoured. CO2-rich atmospheres at the surface pressures of Mars and Venus (about 0.006 and 92 bar) are weakly disfavoured at 2 σ (Glidden 2025).
  • “Our results weakly disfavor CO2-rich atmospheres for pressures corresponding to the surface of Venus and Mars and the cloud tops of Venus at 2 σ.” (Glidden et al. 2025, abstract)

The 3D cosmic shoreline

real Berta-Thompson, Wachiraphan & Murray (2026, ApJ 1011, 123) fit a boundary in three dimensions, escape speed, the flux a planet receives and its star's luminosity, to the planets of the Solar System, to the exoplanets larger than 2.2 Earth radii, which they label as having atmospheres, and to 15 of the 17 planets above (TRAPPIST-1 d and e are not in it, and two of the 15, LHS 3844 b and GJ 1252 b, are Spitzer results). Eqs. 1 to 4 of the paper:

fshore / f⊕ = f0 (vesc / vesc,⊕)p (L / L☉)q
Δ = log10(f / fshore)
P(atmosphere) = 1 / (1 + eΔ / w)

real The survey does what the paper does for its Table 1: it averages P over the paper's posterior, 2,000 draws of the 200,000 the authors published (Zenodo record 15858798, licence CC BY 4.0), so the four constants carry their spread and their correlation. With the paper's own inputs it reproduces Table 1 within 1 percentage point for all nine planets listed there; the largest gap is TOI-771 b, 22.6% against 21.6%.

TRAPPIST-1 and Proxima b are drawn without air

real The paper finds the boundary steep enough to disfavour atmospheres on Earth-sized planets in the habitable zone of stars fainter than log10(L / L☉) = −2.23, about spectral type M4V. TRAPPIST-1 (log L = −3.26 in the archive) and Proxima Centauri (−2.82) are fainter. So TRAPPIST-1 e, f and g, Proxima b and Teegarden's Star b, drawn with seas or ice before this rule (commit c942fc9), are now drawn as airless rock: for each, the posterior's more probable side. Each can still be drawn with thin air, or with thick air, which has seas where the temperature allows them, from the scenarios and sources under its name.

real The line under the name gives the chance of air with its spread, for TRAPPIST-1 e "Chance of air 1% (0.05 to 2%)", and its scenarios and sources say it in full: "Chance it kept an atmosphere, by the 2026 fit, extrapolated: 1% (68% range 0.05 to 2%)". The habitable zone describes the temperature the star gives, and says nothing about whether the planet kept air. Where a world's 68% range crosses 50%, as Ross 128 b's does (19 to 66%), the line says the fit cannot decide, and the picture still takes the more probable side. Where the mass is a minimum mass, as Proxima b's and Teegarden's Star b's are, the chance is a lower bound.

How a bare rock is drawn, and how well it matches

real A bare rock with one face to its star is drawn from its star: the hottest point is Tss = Teff (R★ / a)1/2 (1 − AB)1/4 with a dark surface of Bond albedo 0.1, the temperature falls as the fourth root of the cosine from that point, and the night side is 50 K. Rock is open melt within arccos[(1,673 K / Tss)4] of the point under the star (Kite et al. 2016, eq. 1). Its glow under the star is what it emits there, in the eye's share of each spectrum: 0.9 of the starlight it takes in when it is drawn from the model, and that times (T / Tss)4 when it is drawn at a measured day side.

real The model against every measured day side in the table. For the worlds drawn bare it is within 2.5 σ of each measurement but one; the largest gaps are TRAPPIST-1 c, 2.4 σ above the 369 K of its phase curve (Gillon et al. 2025, a preprint; one eclipse gave 380 ± 31 K), TOI-1468 b, which its paper calls marginally hotter than expected, LHS 3844 b against JWST's 985 K (Kreidberg et al. 2019 measured 1,040 ± 40 K with Spitzer), and LTT 1445 A b, 3.0 σ above its 525 K, because the archive's composite star is hotter than the one its paper used (that paper's own bare-rock maximum is 551 K). For 55 Cnc e and TOI-561 b the measurement is far below the model: that is the sign of their air. Every world in this table is drawn at its measured day side.

illustrative On 55 Cnc e and TOI-561 b that drawing means less. Their measured temperature is that of the layer that emits, which in a thick atmosphere can sit far above the ground: "the photosphere pressure for the thermal emission in 4-12 μm may well be smaller than the surface pressure by orders of magnitude" (Hu et al. 2024, Methods, p. 22). The survey still draws the ground's glow at that temperature, which keeps 55 Cnc e dark: at about 2,000 K, Hu et al. put its thermal emission in the CHEOPS visible band at under 3 ppm (Methods, p. 26), and its lava reflects little.

What changed across the survey

Of the 6,464 planets of the snapshot, 1,956 are drawn with a surface. They are drawn with bare rock 377 times, thin air 1,166 times and thick air 413 times; 17 of them by their own observation.

real Fewer worlds are drawn airless, and more are drawn as thin-air deserts. The fit is more generous than the rule it replaced for planets of bright stars: for a Sun-like star the boundary sits at 417 times Earth's flux for Earth's escape speed, so most hot rocks of FGK stars keep some air by it, while the rocks of faint red dwarfs lose theirs. That regime is the least tested: every rocky exoplanet the fit labels airless orbits an M dwarf, so for the rocks of Sun-like stars the fit rests on the Solar System alone.

Where this can be wrong

  • The fit's probability carries the spread of its constants and not that of each planet's own values: a forecast mass has a scatter the probability does not show. real
  • No observation says how thick the air of an unobserved planet is. The thickness drawn is the survey's own amount, held inside the band of the scenario. illustrative
  • Most exclusions hold for the gases a paper modelled. CO2 is excluded tightly; oxygen and nitrogen, which have no band in the 15 µm filter, are much less constrained (Allen et al. 2025 could not rule out O2 on LTT 3780 b). real
  • TRAPPIST-1 b keeps thick air as disfavoured, not excluded: a pure-CO2 atmosphere with a haze that inverts its temperature fits both of its eclipses (Ducrot et al. 2025). It is drawn bare because the simplest reading of the first eclipses is little or no atmosphere (Greene et al. 2023). real
  • The survey's stellar values are the archive's composite ones, which differ from those each paper used. The bare-rock model's day side therefore differs from each paper's own maximum by a few percent. real
  • Masses not measured are taken from the paper's relation or the Chen & Kipping forecast, and a planet found by radial velocity has only a minimum mass, so its escape speed is uncertain and its probability a lower bound. real
  • The label of no atmosphere the fit learned from covers atmospheres under about 1 to 10 bar, so a planet the fit calls airless may hold thin air, and the drawing cannot show the difference. real
  • The six planets the paper lists without a verdict are decided by the fit here, though each has an observation. real
