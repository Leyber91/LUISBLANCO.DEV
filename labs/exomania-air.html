Which rocky worlds keep air

The survey draws every rocky planet in one of three ways: as bare rock, with thin air, or with thick air. This page says how it chooses, from the code that does it (labs/assets/js/exoplanet/air.js and data.js). Every paper below was opened and read on 16 November 2026. real

plate a · the rule

Observed first, then extrapolated

real Each rocky world carries three scenarios. Each scenario has a status: excluded by an observation, observed, or allowed.

bare rock
No atmosphere.
thin air
An atmosphere too thin to carry the day side's heat to the night side. From outside it looks like bare rock, unless it holds a gas with a strong band in the filter, such as CO2 at 15 µm.
thick air
An atmosphere that carries heat to the night side, or a deck of cloud. Koll (2022) finds that this starts once the surface pressure passes about 1 bar.
  1. Observed first. A rocky planet with its own published observation has each scenario set by its paper. The picture draws the observed scenario if there is one; else the one the paper concludes for; else, among those the paper allows, the more probable by the fit.
  2. Extrapolated for the rest. Every other rocky planet is drawn by the fit of Berta-Thompson, Wachiraphan & Murray (2026), which gives the probability P that a planet holds an atmosphere at all. Bare rock takes 1 − P. Thin air and thick air share P, which the fit does not split. The picture draws bare rock when P is under one half and air otherwise. illustrative How thick the air is drawn comes from the amount of air the survey drew before this rule: escape speed against sunlight, anchored at 7 km/s, which gives Mars 0.47 of Earth's air and Venus 0.82.
  3. Every scenario that is not excluded can be drawn. The line under the planet's name lists the three with their status, and each one that can be drawn instead is a link. An excluded scenario is listed as excluded, with its reason here, and is never drawn.

A world drawn with a surface is one whose radius class is rocky or super-Earth in data.js; a sub-Neptune or anything larger is drawn as cloud tops and has no scenarios.

plate b · why thin air is rarely ruled out

What an eclipse can see

real Almost every observation here is an eclipse: the planet passes behind its star and the drop in light is the planet's own glow. That gives the temperature of the day side. A rock with no atmosphere re-emits sunlight where it lands, so its day side is as hot as it can be: Tday = Tss (2/3)1/4, where Tss is the temperature under the star (Koll 2022, eq. 1). A thick atmosphere moves heat to the night side and the day side comes out cooler. 55 Cnc e is the clearest case: its day side is 1,796 ± 88 K against 2,511 ± 26 K for a black rock with no redistribution (Hu et al. 2024).

real A thin atmosphere moves little heat, so its day side is about as hot as bare rock and the eclipse cannot tell them apart. The papers can rule one out only when it would hold a gas that absorbs inside the filter. So they exclude thin atmospheres of CO2, down to 10 mbar of pure CO2 on LHS 1140 c (Fortune et al. 2025) and 0.01 bar on LTT 3780 b (Allen et al. 2025), and leave many thin atmospheres without it open: oxygen on LTT 3780 b, and about 0.1 bar on GJ 367 b (Zhang et al. 2024). The transits of TRAPPIST-1 d and e are limited by light from the star's own surface that changes from one visit to the next (Piaulet-Ghorayeb et al. 2025; Glidden et al. 2025).

That is why every observed bare rock below keeps thin air as allowed.

Seventeen rocky planets with their own observation

real How the list was made. plate f · the day side of a bare rock

How a bare rock is drawn, and how well it matches

real A bare rock with one face to its star is drawn from its star: the hottest point is Tss = Teff (R★ / a)1/2 (1 − AB)1/4 with a dark surface of Bond albedo 0.1, the temperature falls as the fourth root of the cosine from that point, and the night side is 50 K. Rock is open melt within arccos[(1,673 K / Tss)4] of the point under the star (Kite et al. 2016, eq. 1). Its glow under the star is what it emits there, in the eye's share of each spectrum: 0.9 of the starlight it takes in when it is drawn from the model, and that times (T / Tss)4 when it is drawn at a measured day side.

real The model against every measured day side in the table. For the worlds drawn bare it is within 2.5 σ of each measurement; the largest gaps are TOI-1468 b, which its paper calls marginally hotter than expected, and LHS 3844 b against JWST's 985 K (Kreidberg et al. 2019 measured 1,040 ± 40 K with Spitzer). For 55 Cnc e and TOI-561 b the measurement is far below the model: that is the sign of their air. Every world in this table is drawn at its measured day side.

The fit is more generous than the rule it replaced for planets of bright stars: for a Sun-like star the boundary sits at 417 times Earth's flux for Earth's escape speed, so most hot rocks of FGK stars keep some air by it, while the rocks of faint red dwarfs lose theirs.

Where this can be wrong

  • The probabilities use the fit's medians, so they are sharper than the paper's posterior. illustrative
  • No observation says how thick the air of an unobserved planet is. The thickness drawn is the survey's own amount, held inside the band of the scenario. illustrative
  • Most exclusions hold for the gases a paper modelled. CO2 is excluded tightly; oxygen and nitrogen, which have no band in the 15 µm filter, are much less constrained (Allen et al. 2025 could not rule out O2 on LTT 3780 b). real
  • TRAPPIST-1 b keeps thick air as allowed: a pure-CO2 atmosphere with a haze that inverts its temperature fits both of its eclipses (Ducrot et al. 2025). It is drawn bare because the simplest reading of the first eclipses is little or no atmosphere (Greene et al. 2023). real
  • The survey's stellar values are the archive's composite ones, which differ from those each paper used. The bare-rock model's day side therefore differs from each paper's own maximum by a few percent. real
  • Masses not measured are forecast from the radius, and a planet found by radial velocity has only a minimum mass, so its escape speed is uncertain. real
  • The six planets the paper lists without a verdict are decided by the fit here, though each has an observation. real
