THE LAB · L212
the planner decides · the model narrates

A training block that re-plans itself around the week you actually had.

A deterministic planner turns your days, minutes, kit and flags into a periodized block: sets per muscle inside a time budget, lifts moved along a substitution graph when a flag rules them out, and every session's load run through a fitness-fatigue model. Log a missed or a hard session and the rest of the block re-plans while the curves move. A language model is allowed one job, to explain the planner's own decision log, and each of its sentences is checked against that log.

      09the load model, written out

      load = session RPE × minutes Foster et al. 2001, CR-10 scale, AU

      F(t) = F(t−1)·e−1/τ1 + k1·w(t) fitness, Banister et al. 1975

      H(t) = H(t−1)·e−1/τ2 + k2(t)·w(t) fatigue

      form = F − H

      k2(t) = k3 · Σ w(s)·e−(t−s)/τ3 variable dose, Busso 2003

      parameters
      sensitivity · form with τ1 and τ2 each ±50%

      • [real] session load as RPE × minutes; the fitness-fatigue, variable-dose and 42/7-day chart equations; frequency, rest and the 2-10% load step from ACSM 2009; RPE as reps in reserve from Zourdos et al. 2016; the taper from Bosquet et al. 2007; the low-intensity share from Seiler 2010; WHO 2020 minutes; the knee-force trend from Escamilla 2001; the 876-exercise open library; every number the planner computes from them
      • [illustrative] the model parameters, which are population defaults and not fitted to you; the per-level set targets, rep zones per day, deload size, set and rest timing, exercise attributes, substitution edges and the name screen for open-library entries, which are coaching conventions or lab assumptions chosen to make the mechanism visible
      • [contested] the 10% week-on-week rise flag, a popular convention that a graded programme built on it did not support (Buist et al. 2008); the acute:chronic workload ratio, shown with its critique; neither drives a rule
