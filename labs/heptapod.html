the day

A day is forty acts, and the being writes a ring about each one.

Beyond the glass a being stands in the dark. It writes each whole thought as one ring of ink, in a disc of fog that hangs on its side of the glass. Take a thing from the tray, hold it up, set it down or pass it through the glass: each of these is one act, and it writes one ring about it. A ring gathers out of the fog in about two seconds, all of it at once, hangs for four, then thins from its lightest stroke to its heaviest. Drag across it to keep a piece. The ring in the disc when you arrive is its greeting, and it stays until your first act. Most of its words cut the world differently from English illustrative. In the evening it writes six rings about things you did not do, and your notebook is all you have to read them.

evidence

What is real here, and what is made up to show the mechanism.

  • [REAL] The fiction. Ted Chiang, "Story of Your Life", first published in Starlight 2, ed. Patrick Nielsen Hayden (Tor, 1998). A linguist learns a written language whose units carry whole meanings, by working with its writers across a barrier. The film Arrival (2016) adapted the story; its logograms, its creatures and its staging belong to the film's makers. No image of the film is used or traced here. One value on this page comes from film stills: the densest ink in the disc lets through 45% of the fog's light, set just under the 49% to 51% measured at the densest ink on three stills, and recorded as a calibration in pod/medium.js.
  • [REAL] The problem. W. V. O. Quine, Word and Object (MIT Press, 1960), chapter 2. A linguist hears "gavagai" as a rabbit runs past. That one scene cannot say whether the word means the rabbit, a part of it or a moment of it. More scenes narrow the reading and never close it.
  • [REAL] How people narrow it. Chen Yu and Linda B. Smith, "Rapid word learning under uncertainty via cross-situational statistics", Psychological Science 18(5), 2007: adults who saw several words with several objects at once, where no single trial settled a pairing, learned the pairings from what recurred across trials. Linda B. Smith and Chen Yu, Cognition 106(3), 1558-1568, 2008, found the same in infants of 12 and 14 months.
  • [REAL] The nearest precedent. In the Talking Heads experiment of Luc Steels and colleagues (1999 to 2001), robotic cameras built a shared vocabulary by guessing games about shapes on a board. Both sides of that experiment were machines.
  • [ILLUSTRATIVE] The being and its language. Invented for this lab. A species starts from the twelve English concepts of the tray (two parties, four acts, six things) and changes them by three operations: it splits a word by something English ignores (a thing by where it is, an act by the size of what is handled, "you" by whether the hand was empty), it joins two words into one, or it makes a word that crosses two. The three species used here have 8, 13 and 14 concepts, and between 79% and 100% of them are no English word. The notebook's table is the visitor's evidence laid out by thing and act; it is a design of this lab. The being's body is grown from the species' seed and means nothing more: a stalk on a broad foot, fronds that alternate sides up it (the distichous arrangement of grasses; Roger V. Jean, Phyllotaxis: a systemic study in plant morphogenesis, Cambridge University Press, 1994), and a head that its neck turns toward what it attends to. It has 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10 fronds and never seven, they leave the stalk at different heights, and none comes near the floor, so it is never a radial body standing on its limbs (tests/chamber.test.mjs checks the count, the heights and the floor on 403 grown bodies, and that the silhouette is not radial on 23 of them).
  • [REAL] Why these three species. A proxy learner played day one on 80 generated species, 201 times each under two ways of acting. The proxy keeps four guesses per radical and drops a guess that has failed in more than half the scenes since it was made. On the three species used here it matched 3.8 to 4.3 of the 6 evening rings on average, where a reader with no decoder matches 2.0, and most of its days ended at 3 to 5 of 6: 83%, 81% and 81% of them, the highest of the 80 species. In 144 of the 160 cases (species and way of acting) more than half of the proxy's days ended there. How well the proxy stands for a person has not been measured, and no person had played the day when this was written. node labs/assets/js/heptapod/tests/pod.test.mjs --sweep recomputes all of it.
  • [ILLUSTRATIVE] Its reading of your marks. The being counts each mark you raise against the concepts of the ring it had just written, and reads the mark as the concept that was present with it and absent without it (the difference of the two shares). The count is computed here; the being that counts is invented.
  • [ILLUSTRATIVE] The chamber. The staging is this lab's own: a dark room, a sphere of fog beyond the glass where the being's writing forms, the being standing in the dark beside it, and on this side a polished table with six things on it. The fog is one seeded texture drawn at two scales, each carried its own way, and it stirs faster while the being writes. It is the only light in the room. Every surface is lit by one function: the fog's light falls with the square of the distance from its centre and with the cosine of the angle a surface turns from it (Johann Heinrich Lambert, Photometria, 1760), so a thing far from the glass is dimmer than one near it, a thing held up is brighter than one set down, and the being is lit only on its side that faces the disc. A shadow is the share of the fog a thing hides from a point of the table. The air between scatters a little of the fog's light toward you, by the single-scattering airlight of Bo Sun, Ravi Ramamoorthi, Srinivasa Narasimhan and Shree Nayar ("A practical analytic single scattering model for real time rendering", ACM Transactions on Graphics 24(3), 2005), here without its attenuation. The things are solids traced through each pixel and lit by the same function, and their glaze reflects the fog by Christophe Schlick's approximation of Fresnel reflectance (Computer Graphics Forum 13(3), 1994). The room's depths, its exposure and the materials are design values, and nothing the fog lights is brighter than the disc's own edge (tests/chamber.test.mjs).
  • [ILLUSTRATIVE] The ink. A ring is solved as one curve by the script's solver, so it has no first stroke. In the disc the ink is a field of density on a grid of cells, and the fog's light passes through it by Beer-Lambert absorption, never falling below 45% of the fog's light. A ring arrives as a haze holding all of its ink, and every cell moves toward the ring at one rate, so the whole ring forms at once: with no haze every part would reach half its darkness at the same moment, and with it the parts of a ring get there within a tenth of the two seconds, measured on each species' greeting and on a ring written over another, with the flow the chamber uses (tests/medium.test.mjs). The ink of the ring before leaves on its own, and the flow is still while a ring gathers. While it hangs, a slow flow moves its edges and the pull back to the ring keeps its shape; the flow is the curl of a smooth noise (Robert Bridson, Jim Hourihan and Marcus Nordenstam, "Curl-noise for procedural fluid flow", ACM Transactions on Graphics 26(3), 2007), applied by semi-Lagrangian advection (Jos Stam, "Stable Fluids", SIGGRAPH 1999). The being presses harder on the concept it has written least often, and the thinning removes the same width from every stroke each second, so what it pressed on is the last thing in the disc. The two seconds of forming and the four of hanging are design values; with motion reduced, a ring appears whole, stays nine seconds and goes in three steps.
  • [REAL] The evening's chance level. Matching one of three pictures six times with no decoder gives 2 of 6 on average and 3 or more in 32 tries of 100 (binomial, n = 6, p = 1/3), computed on this page. The three pictures are all scenes you did not do, so remembering your day does not help: a reader who only rules out its own acts scores 2.0 in the same test of the proxy.
  • [REAL] Where your day is kept. The notebook, the guesses and the marks stay in this browser, in the labs' own store, and the privacy page can export or erase them. If you have allowed sharing of usage, the author receives: that this page was opened and for how long, each act (its kind and its number in the day), each crop (how many radicals it kept), each mark raised (as a count), and the evening's summary (which of the three species, how many rings, radicals kept and marks, and the score). Nothing you type is in any of it. The page uses no model.
  • [REAL] The script. Its solver, its radicals and a reader that recovers a ring from pixels are on the bench page, with their own sources.
