contact · day one

A day with one being, and no dictionary.

Behind the glass is a being that writes each whole thought as one ring. Take a thing from the tray, hold it up, set it down or pass it through the glass: each of these is one act, and it writes one ring about it. A ring forms in about two seconds, hangs for four, then thins from its lightest stroke to its heaviest. Drag across it to keep a piece. The ring on the glass when you arrive is its greeting, and it stays until your first act. Most of its words cut the world differently from English illustrative.

This is a recorded day, replayed from a seed. Nothing on it is stored. Start your own day.

This browser is not keeping the labs' storage, so your day lasts until this tab closes.

evidence

What is real here, and what is made up to show the mechanism.

  • [REAL] The fiction. Ted Chiang, "Story of Your Life", first published in Starlight 2, ed. Patrick Nielsen Hayden (Tor, 1998). A linguist learns a written language whose units carry whole meanings, by working with its writers across a barrier. The film Arrival (2016) adapted the story; its logograms and its creatures belong to the film's makers and nothing here is drawn from them.
  • [REAL] The problem. W. V. O. Quine, Word and Object (MIT Press, 1960), chapter 2. A linguist hears "gavagai" as a rabbit runs past. That one scene cannot say whether the word means the rabbit, a part of it or a moment of it. More scenes narrow the reading and never close it.
  • [REAL] How people narrow it. Chen Yu and Linda B. Smith, "Rapid word learning under uncertainty via cross-situational statistics", Psychological Science 18(5), 2007: adults who saw several words with several objects at once, where no single trial settled a pairing, learned the pairings from what recurred across trials. Linda B. Smith and Chen Yu, Cognition 106(3), 1558-1568, 2008, found the same in infants of 12 and 14 months.
  • [REAL] The nearest precedent. In the Talking Heads experiment of Luc Steels and colleagues (1999 to 2001), robotic cameras built a shared vocabulary by guessing games about shapes on a board. Both sides of that experiment were machines.
  • [ILLUSTRATIVE] The being and its language. Invented for this lab. A species starts from the twelve English concepts of the tray (two parties, four acts, six things) and changes them by three operations: it splits a word by something English ignores (a thing by where it is, an act by the size of what is handled, "you" by whether the hand was empty), it joins two words into one, or it makes a word that crosses two. The three species used here have 8, 13 and 14 concepts, and between 79% and 100% of them are no English word. The notebook's table is the visitor's evidence laid out by thing and act; it is a design of this lab. The body in the fog is drawn from the species' seed and means nothing more.
  • [REAL] Why these three species. A proxy learner played day one on 80 generated species, 201 times each under two ways of acting. The proxy keeps four guesses per radical and drops a guess that has failed in more than half the scenes since it was made. On the three species used here it matched 3.8 to 4.3 of the 6 evening rings on average, where a reader with no decoder matches 2.0, and most of its days ended at 3 to 5 of 6: 83%, 81% and 81% of them, the highest of the 80 species. In 144 of the 160 cases (species and way of acting) more than half of the proxy's days ended there. How well the proxy stands for a person has not been measured, and no person had played the day when this was written. node labs/assets/js/heptapod/tests/pod.test.mjs --sweep recomputes all of it.
  • [ILLUSTRATIVE] Its reading of your marks. The being counts each mark you raise against the concepts of the ring it had just written, and reads the mark as the concept that was present with it and absent without it (the difference of the two shares). The count is computed here; the being that counts is invented.
  • [ILLUSTRATIVE] The ink. A ring is solved as one curve by the script's solver, so it has no first stroke. The being presses harder on the concept it has written least often, and the thinning removes the same width from every stroke each second, so what it pressed on is the last thing on the glass. The two seconds of forming and the four of hanging are design values; with motion reduced, a ring appears whole, stays nine seconds and goes in three steps.
  • [REAL] The evening's chance level. Matching one of three pictures six times with no decoder gives 2 of 6 on average and 3 or more in 32 tries of 100 (binomial, n = 6, p = 1/3), computed on this page. The three pictures are all scenes you did not do, so remembering your day does not help: a reader who only rules out its own acts scores 2.0 in the same test of the proxy.
  • [REAL] Where your day is kept. The notebook, the guesses and the marks stay in this browser, in the labs' own store, and the privacy page can export or erase them. If you have allowed sharing of usage, the author receives: that this page was opened and for how long, each act (its kind and its number in the day), each crop (how many radicals it kept), each mark raised (as a count), and the evening's summary (which of the three species, how many rings, radicals kept and marks, and the score). Nothing you type is in any of it. The page uses no model.
  • [REAL] The script. Its solver, its radicals and a reader that recovers a ring from pixels are on the bench page, with their own sources.
